# Tasuki Keifu SEO: Current State and Next Observations

## 1. Purpose

This is an explanation of recent search-engine work, not a code design. It records what had been changed, why sitemap and search-result entry points mattered, how to observe the effects, and which outcomes still needed time to become clear.

## 2. Main Direction

The goal of this round was to make the site's existing structure easier for search engines to understand. Three basics mattered:

- Give crawlers a reliable sitemap entry point.
- Lead search visitors to clear, readable, clickable pages.
- Make important existing pages recognizable as trustworthy entity pages.

Only after these basics are sound does it make sense to expect healthier indexing, impressions, and clicks.

## 3. Changes Completed in This Round

### 3.1 Sitemap Index and Shards

The main sitemap entry became `https://tasukikeifu.com/sitemap-index.xml`. It points to separate sitemaps for static pages, athletes, races, and organizations. This makes it easier for crawlers to read pages by type, gives the structure room to grow, and helps distinguish which group changed after an update.

### 3.2 `robots.txt`

`robots.txt` was updated to declare the new sitemap index instead of an older single sitemap. Crawlers using that file should therefore reach the current sitemap structure directly.

### 3.3 Readable Athlete URLs

Some old athlete URLs worked but were hard to interpret in search results. For a subset with a safely verified one-to-one mapping, redirects were added to the readable canonical athlete pages. This did not change the athlete content; it made the destination of a search result easier to recognize.

### 3.4 Stronger Signals on Important Pages

Titles, summaries, links between key pages, and structured information were improved on athlete, race, and organization details, as well as list and entry pages. The intent was to help a crawler identify the entities the site describes, the relationships among pages, and the best landing pages for searches.

## 4. Why Sitemap Counts May Fluctuate

Google Search Console briefly showed zero discovered pages after the sitemap entry was re-read. That number alone did not establish that the sitemap was broken. One possible explanation was that Google had recognized the parent as a sitemap index before the child sitemap totals had appeared in the interface.

At the time of the notes, the more useful checks were whether the index and child sitemaps could be fetched and whether crawling of the new structure had resumed. Those basic checks were reported as healthy. Console numbers can lag the live state of a site.

## 5. Google and Bing Status

Google had re-read the new sitemap structure. The next signals to watch were the parent index totals, the discovered-page counts from child sitemaps, and whether indexing and impressions stabilized after re-reading.

The new sitemap entry had also been submitted to Bing. Its refresh speed, replacement of old statistics, and long-tail page performance still needed observation.

## 6. How to Judge the Work

Traffic on the same day was not the right success measure. Over the following period, the useful signals were:

- Continued successful sitemap reads.
- Recovery or growth in indexed pages.
- Continued impressions for long-tail athlete pages.
- Readable pages gradually replacing old URL forms in search results.
- More stable click-through rates for athlete, race, and organization pages.

The main question was whether search engines understood the site's structure more consistently. More traffic would only be a meaningful result after that became visible in the data.

## 7. Next Steps

The proposed next steps were limited and verifiable: redirect a few more old athlete links when mappings were certain, inspect pages with impressions but weak click-through rates, improve search snippets without changing business meaning, and keep sitemap and crawl entry points stable.

The original plan did not call for a broad product redesign, mass-produced content, or inflating the number of pages to chase search metrics.

## 8. Recommendation at the Time

Keep the sitemap structure stable, observe how Google and Bing fill in their reports, and make small search-presentation changes with clear evidence. Unless a new problem appears, the work should move into monitoring and incremental improvement rather than another structural overhaul.
